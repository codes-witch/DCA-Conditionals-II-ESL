\section{Discussion and conclusion}

Observing the high dispersion of type II conditionals across task topics reveals clear task effects:
the number of conditionals elicited by each task is indeed not proportional to what would be expected simply from the number of words in their responses.
Similar conclusions have been reached regarding the EFCAMDAT not in terms of a specific construction, but with a focus on different measures of linguistic complexity \citep{alexopoulouTaskEffect2017}.
However, perhaps surprisingly, this study reports the opposite pattern regarding level: second conditionals appear to be produced in similar proportions by students in lower and higher levels of proficiency.
